# Website audit rubric for local-business leads

Use this as a short, evidence-first audit. The goal is to uncover a practical improvement, not to produce a vague critique.

## 1. Availability and trust

- Does the homepage load?
- Does the site use HTTPS without an observed browser warning?
- Are the business identity, service area and basic contact details visible?
- Is there a visible privacy or legal page where appropriate? Record only what is observed.

## 2. Mobile usability

Check at a typical mobile viewport:

- navigation works
- text can be read without zooming
- buttons have enough space and can be tapped
- no important horizontal overflow
- images and key content remain visible

## 3. Conversion path

A local business should let a visitor understand the offer and take a next step quickly.

Look for:

- one clear primary action: WhatsApp, phone, booking, quote or visit
- a primary action visible early on the page
- contact action repeated at useful points
- an easy path from service to enquiry
- a short form only when it is useful

## 4. Offer clarity and trust

Check whether a new visitor can identify:

- primary services
- customer type or problem solved
- service area
- reasons to choose the business: portfolio, testimonials, certifications, team, process or clear information

## 5. Technical signals, only when actually tested

When tools are available, record the method, date and result for:

- HTTPS
- redirects and broken pages
- mobile layout observation
- Lighthouse or PageSpeed performance/accessibility diagnostics

Do not translate a single audit score into a broad commercial claim. Say what the test measured and recommend the next improvement.

## Mini-audit format

| Category | Observation | Evidence | Customer impact | Smallest useful fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Contact path | WhatsApp link is not visible on the homepage | URL + page note | Visitor may need to search for how to ask for a quote | Add fixed WhatsApp CTA and contact section |

## Recommended language

- “I noticed … when I checked the homepage today.”
- “A visitor on mobile may have trouble … because …”
- “A simple improvement would be …”
- “This is a suggestion based on a quick public review, not a full technical audit.”
